B2. BANK RESERVE AND DAILY ECONOMY DASHBOARD

Owner's requirements:
- The bank starts with its own reserve of J$100,000, recorded exactly once.
- A reserve below 90% of that original amount is marked dangerous.
  Threshold: J$90,000. Exactly J$90,000 is not below the threshold.
- At 12 pm each day, publish an economy snapshot for the console's Joe Bank
  section: total money in the market, total assets, average member asset value
  and percentage change, tax revenue, bank reserves and other essential metrics.
- Target average member value around J$10,000; use this as a monitoring target.
- Recommend an interest-rate change using reserve conditions; final authority
  remains with the operator. Never automatically apply a recommendation.

Design defaults and definitions:
- "12 pm" means NOON America/New_York, not midnight. Store timestamps in UTC.
  Use a New York date key for a once-only daily snapshot, including DST changes.
  Delayed snapshots show actual capture time and any stale/incomplete status;
  do not fabricate a historic noon value from later balances.
- Separate member posted cash, member available cash, active holds, company
  spendable cash, bank-owned reserves, locked founding capital and fee clearing.
  A hold is part of posted money, not additional money to add a second time.
- Total member asset value = reference-valued ordinary holdings plus frozen
  shareholdings. Show average asset value over eligible members and percentage
  difference from J$10,000: (average - 10,000) / 10,000 x 100.
- Also show average total member portfolio value INCLUDING posted cash/holds,
  with a separate label. The existing disposable-only net-position card cannot
  be reused as a complete wealth statistic because held cash would disappear.
  Do not silently replace the owner's asset-value metric with this second metric.
- Exclude bank/issuer/operator/system accounts from the member denominator.
  Return an empty/not-applicable average when there are no eligible members.
- Show week-to-date taxes, loan interest actually received, deposit interest paid,
  employee payments, redistribution, opening grants, refunds and reserve ratio.
- Reconcile totals across service snapshots at a declared common cutoff or mark
  the result provisional. Do not present independently captured totals as exact.
- Keep bank-owned reserve funds separate from member deposits, company money,
  founding capital and trading fee balances. None is freely spendable by the bank
  merely because Banking stores the ledger account.
- The J$90,000 condition is a warning, not an unapproved automatic seizure,
  forced liquidation, rate change or trading suspension. Spending-floor rules
  and redistribution budgets still need a precise policy.
- No automatic stock-price manipulation to force averages back to J$10,000.

B3. COMPANY SUBSCRIPTIONS, PURCHASES AND EMPLOYEE INCOME

Preserve the owner's model: a company can launch subscription/purchase services
through an API. ChatJoePT can link a member to a subscription checkout, generating
company revenue. Part goes to EMPLOYEES; the remainder stays with the company
and contributes to its economic value.

Essential fit with the existing trade system:
- All cash must be real posted J$ within Banking, never a second exchange balance.
  Create company operating/revenue accounts separate from locked founding capital.
- A paid purchase debits the member and credits the company once. Employee wages
  subsequently debit company cash and credit the designated employees once.
  Retained cash is the remainder; do not credit it again as newly created money.
- Revenue can increase company book assets through retained cash. Stock price
  remains an output of the existing market engine. Any new revenue/fundamental
  input must be bounded, attributed and documented; never directly write prices
  or add the same revenue simultaneously as cash and a second fictitious asset.
- Distinguish company book value from market capitalization (reference share
  price x actual outstanding shares). Do not add company treasury assets again
  to a system-wide total already valuing that company through its shares.
- A stock trade between members pays the seller, not company revenue. Founding
  capital and loan proceeds are funding, not sales revenue. Share sales do not
  mint money, and taxes/transfers/wages normally move existing money.
- Existing member wires stay receiver-approved. Purchases/subscriptions are a
  separate buyer-authorized payment flow; a merchant link alone cannot debit J$.

Minimal payment and subscription design:
- Approved JoeAuth service/app identity linked to the correct company; server-
  determined merchant account, plan, price and allowed service permissions.
- Member checkout shows merchant, amount, one-time/recurring terms and consent.
  Recurring billing needs explicit authorization, interval, next charge, and a
  cancel action that stops future charges. Price changes require defined consent.
- Durable purchase/renewal identifiers, sufficient-available-funds checks, balanced
  postings and signed/deduplicated delivery of payment/entitlement results.
- Grant the in-universe entitlement only after payment is durably recorded.
  Retries, declined charges and outages cannot grant duplicate access or debit twice.
- Agree refund, missed-renewal and employee-payment timing before enabling billing.
  Refunds must link to original payments and correct revenue/tax history explicitly.
- Managers configure employees and the approved payout rule. Never let an employee
  choose their own percentage or let payouts exceed settled company funds.
- Revenue reporting is weekly and lifetime, with gross receipts, refunds, employee
  payouts and retained amount separately visible.

Still to confirm: employee payout percentage, split among employees, eligibility,
manual versus scheduled payout, employment acceptance, subscription intervals,
refund rules and revenue-to-market signal. These are not supplied by Dev_Note;
do not invent them as approved policy.

B4. WEEKLY INCOME TAX — PRESERVE THE OWNER'S BRACKET MODEL

The owner's example applies the selected bracket rate to the WHOLE weekly taxable
income. Keep that model; do not quietly replace it with progressive marginal tax.
Make the threshold jumps visible in the design preview.

Weekly taxable income I (J$)       Rate applied to the whole I
0 <= I <= 500                     0%
500 < I <= 2,000                   2%
2,000 < I <= 3,500                 3%
3,500 < I <= 5,000                 5%
I > 5,000                         10%

Essential arithmetic correction:
J$1,000 income + J$600 stock exchange GAIN = J$1,600 taxable income.
J$1,600 x 2% = J$32, not the J$80 written in the source example.
Keep the stated rates; correcting the multiplication does not change tax policy.

Essential accounting fit:
- Use a New York calendar week, proposed Monday 00:00 through the following
  Monday 00:00, as a half-open interval; explicitly confirm this week convention.
- Tax income and realized trading GAINS, not the full proceeds of every sale or
  an unrealized increase in the displayed portfolio price. A J$600 sale is not
  necessarily a J$600 gain. Define sale income using proceeds, allocated cost
  basis and fees before finalizing the taxable basis.
- Preserve exact cost-basis records for purchases, founder contributions and
  private share transfers. The current rounded average-per-share display is
  insufficient by itself for exact tax calculations; plan durable basis/lot data
  and a reviewed transition for existing holdings without inventing past profit.
- Exclude transfers of principal between members/own accounts, starting grants,
  loans and capital contributions from taxable earned income by default. Record
  the category of every payment to avoid mistaking a loan or wire for wages.
- Finalize whether deposit interest, grants, redistribution and dividends count,
  whether trading losses offset gains or wages, fee deductions, refund handling,
  and whether frozen-share gains use the same basis rule. These remain policy
  questions, not assumptions hidden in a scheduled debit.
- Assess tax once per member/week with an immutable calculation and source links.
  Collection is a balanced member-to-bank-reserve transfer with independent
  idempotency; never tax the same wage/trade twice on retry.
- Handle insufficient disposable funds explicitly. Do not spend existing wire/
  order holds, make available balances negative, liquidate frozen shares, or debit
  a different member. Choose withholding, an assessed unpaid balance, or another
  explicit policy in Stage 1 before automatic collection is activated.
- Reconcile assessments, payments, refunds and unpaid tax separately. Tax receipts
  must reach the bank-owned reserve, not the exchange's ordinary fee account.

B5. MONEY SUPPLY, REDISTRIBUTION AND RATE RECOMMENDATIONS

Owner requirements:
- Eligible new members receive J$10,000 disposable balance exactly once.
- Redistribute some collected taxes and bank interest income to support money
  circulation and reduce inequality.
- Start the bank reserve at J$100,000; warn below J$90,000.
- Recommend rate changes from reserve conditions; the operator makes the change.

Reconcile with the current system:
- Register the opening grant once per verified, eligible identity linked through
  JoeAuth and Banking. Linking an old account, switching apps or signing in again
  must not create a second J$10,000. Enrollment/proof-based migration is still work.
- Specify whether opening grants debit the J$100,000 reserve or use separately
  authorized issuance. Funding ten full grants from that reserve would exhaust
  it; do not silently choose a funding source that defeats the owner's threshold.
- Company sales/wages circulate existing J$; they do not replenish system-wide
  spendable money locked by new founding contributions. Redistribution returns
  bank-owned funds to members. Publish sources, payments and remaining budgets.
- Treat only collected loan interest as bank interest income; principal repayments
  are not income. Accrued but unreceived interest is not cash available to distribute.
  Deposit interest paid to members is a bank outflow, not bank revenue.
- Keep a defined redistribution budget and eligibility rule; determine cadence,
  equal versus targeted allocation, treatment of new/inactive members and the
  reserve warning/spending policy before implementing payouts.
- No daily allowances, contribution bounties or other earlier brainstorm ideas
  are automatically approved by this note. The chosen owner mechanism is company
  income, employee payments, taxation and redistribution.
- Show an explainable recommendation using reserve ratio, trend, interest received
  and interest expense. Reserve ratio alone cannot prove that raising a shared
  deposit/loan rate will improve reserves: it changes both income and obligations.
  Do not hard-code "low reserve = raise the rate" without a defined model.
- Recommend a bounded signed step only after a rule is approved; otherwise show
  "insufficient policy/data". Operator approval remains mandatory and follows B6.

B6. JOE BANK CONSOLE — RATE OPERATIONS AND LOANS FROM THE HANDOFF

Keep the compatible handoff requirements even though Dev_Note emphasizes the
new economy controls. Include dashboard, reserves/tax/redistribution views,
rate adjustment/history/scheduled operations and loan creation under Joe Bank.

Rates:
- Button opens a modal with operation date AND time, New York timezone, signed
  change, current/proposed rate, reason, member explanation and internal note.
- Minimum absolute adjustment 0.005 percentage points; maximum 0.200; step 0.005.
  Nonzero positive and negative changes are allowed.
- Weekly rate bounds 0.300%–2.000%; at most two successful effective adjustments
  in one New York calendar week. Initial rate is unconfirmed; 1.000% is a proposal.
- Exact units: weekly_rate_ppm; step 50 ppm, maximum movement 2,000 ppm;
  rate bounds 3,000–20,000 ppm. All amounts use integer cents.
- Future operations are pending and cancellable. Applied/canceled/failed states,
  policy versions, server-side permissions and audit trail are required.
- Atomically resolve cancel-versus-execute races; validate pending sequences and
  execution-time bounds/quota. Reserve pending weekly slots as a design default,
  releasing them on cancellation/failure, while effective operations count once.
- Never backdate rates. Delayed jobs record their actual effective time. Fixed
  contracts keep accepted rates; variable accrual splits at each effective change.
- Applying a rate operation durably creates one linked structured Joe Wire event.
  Cuts create bullish pressure; increases create bearish pressure. These are
  simulation directions, not guaranteed price moves. Existing market caps and
  news/tick rules still apply; no direct overwrite of stock prices.
- Market impact occurs at successful effective execution, not at both scheduling
  and execution. Cancellation creates no impact. Use retry-safe cross-service
  delivery and retain event attribution and member-visible rate history.
- Decide event severity/duration/affected stocks before implementation; do not
  silently extend the effect to crypto or set a fixed guaranteed return.
- Banking rate-policy permission is explicit; exchange/global JoeAuth operator
  status alone does not grant it. Fixed-point interest accrues by approved
  contract rules and posts weekly without duplicate posting on catch-up.

Loans:
- Keep bank-owned offers/contracts and repayments inside Joe Banking's ledger.
  The Joe Bank console may prepare an offer for an eligible linked member.
- Offer fields: member, principal, purpose, variable/fixed kind, weekly rate,
  term, payment schedule and expiry. Member acceptance of the exact offer is
  required before disbursement or interest; creating an offer alone moves no J$.
- Acceptance posts a balanced transaction and loan receivable exactly once.
  Retain repayment/accrual/history, permission checks and retry protection.
- Principal limits, permitted terms, fixed-rate catalog, minimum payments,
  first due date, grace periods, unpaid interest, concurrent loans and whether
  proceeds may fund JoeSE purchases remain unapproved. Do not invent penalties
  or revive legacy annual-rate lending. Decide whether loan disbursement uses
  reserve-funded transfers or separately authorized issuance; both its reserve
  effect and money-supply effect must be visible. Preserve any accepted legacy
  contracts until a separate migration decision.
